I’m a Ph.D. candidate in Mechanical Engineering at VILAB, KAIST, advised by Prof. Kuk-Jin Yoon. As a computer vision researcher, I study how AI can perceive 3D environments, understand human behaviour, and connect information across different modalities.

My work spans 3D perception and data-efficient learning, including LiDAR/Radar perception, open-world category discovery, and vision–language dataset distillation. Through these topics, I have explored how models can recognize unfamiliar categories and learn useful representations from limited data.

More recently, my research has focused on egocentric understanding and prediction, audio-visual generation, and spatial audio. I’m interested in understanding activities and human motion from sparse wearable signals, as well as generating and controlling sound grounded in visual content. I also explore retrieval and lightweight adaptation as ways to extend pretrained models.

I’m also interested in vision–language models (VLMs) and VLM diagnosis: understanding what pretrained models represent, where their reasoning succeeds or fails, and whether local visual decisions form a coherent global understanding.

My broader goal is to develop efficient, controllable multimodal AI that can be applied to AR/VR and wearable devices. I’m particularly interested in connecting 3D understanding with human-centred perception and generation to support useful, immersive experiences.
